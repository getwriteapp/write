On Quiet Software

A short essay, written in write

Most tools shout. They greet you with ribbons of buttons, panels that fold out into more panels, a dozen ways to do a thing you only need to do one way. They mistake capability for usefulness, and in doing so they put a small wall between you and the page.

The case for less

A word processor has exactly one job: to get out of the way so that you can write. Everything else — the formatting, the exports, the page margins — is in service of that single act. When the software is quiet, the writing gets loud. That is the whole bargain.

The best interface is the one you forget you are using.

What gets in the way

Three things, usually:

  • Chrome that never hides, even when you are mid-sentence.
  • Typography chosen by an engineer, not a reader.
  • A blank page that feels like a form to fill out, not a sheet of paper.

Fix those three and you have already beaten most of the field. You can read more about the design principles elsewhere, but it really does come down to restraint.

A different bargain

So here is the offer: a word processor that opens in half a second, that looks like something you would want to read, that saves your work without ever asking, and that disappears the moment you start to type.
