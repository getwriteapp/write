Open a page. Not a workspace, not a project, not a canvas of blocks — a page. One column, one cursor, and a quiet so complete you can hear the next sentence coming.

Start from quiet

This room is borrowed, gratefully, from iA Writer: the typeface holds the line steady, the cursor is the only color, and nothing on the screen asks to be clicked. But watch what happens when you reach for emphasis — select a few words and a small toolbar finds you, then leaves when you stop needing it. Real bold. Real headings. A real document underneath — one that will leave this app as a clean .docx — not markup wearing a costume.

Make the tool disappear; keep the craft.

Go up from there

Going up doesn't mean adding chrome. It means the capabilities arrive in the order you reach for them:

  • Headings, lists, and quotes that survive the trip to Word and back.
  • Focus that dims everything except the paragraph you're in — try it from the pill above.
  • Rooms instead of themes: each one changes the light, the type, the temperature of the work.

Everything else — margins, exports, print — stays backstage until called for. The page is the interface.

Now you

This page is editable. Click anywhere and type. Select something and make it bold. The app should already be gone from your attention by the time you finish this sentence.
